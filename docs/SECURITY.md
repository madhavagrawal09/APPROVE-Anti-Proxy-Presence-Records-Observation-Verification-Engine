# APPROVE — Security Baseline

**Status:** Baseline requirements for design and implementation. This document is not a security certification.

## 1. Security objectives

- Prevent unauthorized account creation and role escalation.
- Make attendance evidence session-bound, time-bound, and resistant to replay.
- Raise the difficulty of proxy attendance through independent verification signals.
- Preserve a traceable history of attempts, reviews, and corrections.
- Minimize collection and retention of student data.

## 2. Threat model (pilot)

| Threat | Baseline mitigation | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public/self-service account abuse | No public student registration; accounts provisioned by an administrator | Compromised administrator account remains high impact. |
| QR screenshot reuse | Short-lived signed challenge, session binding, single-use nonce / replay tracking | A live relay or collusion may still be possible. |
| Student uses another person's phone | Registered device key and challenge-response | Device possession does not prove the identity of the human holding it. |
| Remote submission | Registered room anchor evidence plus short-lived server validation | BLE RSSI is not cryptographic distance proof; relay attacks remain possible. |
| Client clock manipulation | Server-controlled time and expiry | Network delay and temporary connectivity still require policy decisions. |
| Unauthorized attendance edits | Role-based server authorization and append-only audit events | Privileged database operators remain within the operational trust boundary. |
| Lost or replaced device | Device revocation and controlled re-enrollment | Account recovery needs a verified administrative process. |
| Credential theft | Strong password hashing, secure sessions/tokens, rate limits, and temporary credentials | Phishing and endpoint compromise remain possible. |

## 3. Identity and account provisioning

- Student and faculty accounts are created by authorized administrators.
- Initial credentials are temporary and must be changed at first successful login.
- No permanent default administrator password or hardcoded secret is permitted.
- The first administrator must be bootstrapped through a protected deployment procedure or secret-management facility.
- Passwords must be stored using a modern adaptive password hash; plaintext and reversible password storage are prohibited.
- Login, password reset, device registration, and privileged actions require rate limiting and audit coverage.
- Authorization must be enforced by the backend, not merely by hiding UI controls.

## 4. Device binding

- Each registered device generates a public/private key pair using platform-supported secure key storage where available.
- The private key must remain on the device and must never be uploaded to the backend.
- The backend stores the public key and device lifecycle metadata.
- Sensitive requests use a server-issued challenge and a signature generated by the registered private key.
- Device replacement requires revocation of the old registration and a controlled re-registration flow.
- Device binding establishes possession of a registered key; it does not prove who is physically operating the device.
- Hardware-backed key storage and platform attestation are capability-dependent and must be reported honestly.

## 5. QR challenge requirements

The eventual protocol must specify and test:

- cryptographic signature verification on the backend;
- binding to one attendance session and one challenge generation;
- short expiry evaluated using server time;
- unique challenge identifiers and single-use consumption;
- replay rejection, including concurrent duplicate submissions;
- key rotation and signing-key storage;
- rate limiting and safe error responses.

The QR must not contain student secrets, reusable bearer credentials, or personal data that is unnecessary for verification.

## 6. BLE room-anchor requirements

- Each anchor is explicitly registered to a room by an authorized administrator.
- Anchor messages use authenticated, rotating identifiers or challenge data; static identifiers alone are insufficient.
- The backend validates anchor registration, freshness, and association with the active session.
- Anchor revocation and health monitoring are required.
- Student scanning should occur during the foreground attendance flow, respecting iOS and Android platform constraints.
- RSSI may be recorded as a noisy supporting signal, but it must not be treated as proof of distance.
- Relay attacks are a known residual risk. APPROVE must not be described as “proxy-proof” or as implementing formal distance-bounding.

## 7. Attendance states and human review

- `VERIFIED_PRESENT`: all required verification checks passed.
- `PENDING_REVIEW`: an attendance attempt exists, but evidence is failed, incomplete, or inconclusive.
- `ABSENT`: no attendance attempt exists when the session is closed, subject to the defined session policy.
- `REJECTED`: optional terminal label for an invalid or fraudulent attempt; it must not erase the attempt or its evidence.

Faculty has final authority over pending cases. A review decision and every later correction must record the actor, timestamp, reason, previous value, and new value. The system must not silently overwrite a prior decision.

## 8. Data protection and operations

- Collect only fields necessary for enrollment, session operation, verification, and audit.
- Do not add CCTV, face recognition, or continuous location tracking to the MVP.
- Use TLS for network traffic and encrypt sensitive data at rest through the chosen hosting/database facilities.
- Keep secrets out of source control, logs, screenshots, and client bundles.
- Separate development, test, and production credentials and databases.
- Define retention, deletion, backup, restore, and incident-response procedures before a live institutional pilot.
- Restrict production database and infrastructure access to named operators with least privilege.

## 9. Required security tests before pilot

- expired, malformed, tampered, and wrong-session QR challenge rejection;
- replay and concurrent duplicate-submission rejection;
- revoked and unregistered device rejection;
- unregistered, revoked, stale, and wrong-room anchor rejection;
- student cannot access another student's records or staff operations;
- faculty cannot modify sessions or rosters outside their authorization;
- every review and correction creates an audit event;
- session close marks only no-attempt participants absent;
- secret scanning and dependency vulnerability checks in CI.

## 10. Claims and limitations

Layered verification reduces specific fraud opportunities but cannot guarantee that the enrolled student is physically holding the device. BLE proximity is not secure distance measurement. The product must communicate confidence and review states rather than claiming perfect identity or location assurance.
